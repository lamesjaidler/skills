# Torres Methodology Reference (condensed)

Condensed from web research (Product Talk, chapter summaries, Lenny's Podcast interview) during the design session for the `continuous-discovery` plugin. The skill-writing GREEN phases draw on this; it exists nowhere else on disk. Working reference only — not shipped skill content.

**Definition:** "At a minimum, weekly touchpoints with customers, by the team building the product, where they conduct small research activities in pursuit of a desired outcome." Structural unit: product trio (PM + designer + engineer). Keystone habit: continuous interviewing. Six mindsets: outcome-oriented, customer-centric, collaborative, visual, experimental, continuous.

**Workflow chain:** business outcome → product outcome for a durable trio (quarterly-ish) → experience map of current customer experience (once + refined) → weekly story-based interviews → interview snapshots → OST (outcome / opportunities / solutions / assumption tests; living, per trio per outcome) → top-down sibling comparison to ONE target opportunity → individual-then-share ideation to 3 solutions → story map + pre-mortem → assumptions → 2×2 map → parallel 1–2-day tests with pre-set criteria across all 3 solutions → ship survivors → instrument → does product outcome move, does business outcome follow → loops at every level. All loops run concurrently, never as phases.

**Metric tiers:** business outcome (lagging, company), product outcome (customer behaviour the trio can influence — the OST root), traction metric (single-feature usage; junior teams/late optimisation only).

**Interview snapshot fields:** vivid quote, quick facts, opportunities heard (customer's words), interesting insights, relevant experience-map slice. Snapshot, not raw notes, is the sharing/synthesis unit. (Book includes a photo; drop for markdown.)

**Assumption test techniques:** unmoderated tests, one-question surveys, prototypes, fake-door, wizard-of-oz, data mining. Simulate a moment of the experience where the participant behaves consistently or inconsistently with the assumption. Iterate small/weak → bigger/stronger evidence only for assumptions that keep mattering. Framing: reducing risk, not proving truth.

**The 12 anti-patterns (guardrail file content):**
1. Asking customers what they want / to predict behaviour (use past-story elicitation).
2. Research as a phase, on-demand event, or outsourced to a research team.
3. Validating one idea at a time (confirmation bias, escalation of commitment).
4. Testing ideas instead of underlying assumptions; over-built MVPs where a 1–2-day simulation suffices.
5. Output creep: outcome restated as "ship feature X"; traction metrics as outcomes; multiple simultaneous outcomes.
6. OST malformations: solutions disguised as opportunities; company-perspective opportunities ("increase signups"); vertical branchless trees; two-parent nodes; opportunities without interview evidence; overreacting to n=1.
7. Effort included in opportunity prioritisation.
8. Group brainstorming in one session; idea variations counted as distinct ideas.
9. Percentage test criteria at tiny n; no pre-defined criteria; wrong test audience.
10. Whether-or-not (yes/no) decision framing instead of compare-and-contrast.
11. Sharing raw notes instead of snapshots; telling stakeholders conclusions instead of showing the tree.
12. One-person recruiting bottleneck; waiting for permission to start.
